Real physics, real failures

First physics run: 5 of 20 landed. We traced each failure and fixed it: 24 of 25.

Hardware: we redesigned the wrist so the claw can rotate, still with only five SG90 servos.

(2:40–3:20) A few decisions we’re proud of. We didn’t guess where the camera goes: we rendered 21 camera positions and measured how much of each object stays visible. The surprise was that parking the arm before each snapshot mattered more than camera position: worst-case visibility went from 4% to 100%. The camera calibrates itself from four coloured stickers on the arm’s 25 cm base board, so nobody measures anything. Early detection merged touching cubes into one blob; we split blobs by colour and rejoin a cube’s faces. And when we added real physics, only 5 of 20 cubes landed in the basket at first. We logged every failure — the parked claw hit the basket, cubes stacked and tumbled out, fingers kicked cubes on release — fixed each, and got to 24 of 25. On the hardware side, we redesigned the wrist so the claw rotates, without adding a sixth motor.
