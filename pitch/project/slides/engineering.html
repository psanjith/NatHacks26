Built for a noisy room and a moving head

Clench detection that ignores hum

Eyes tracked through head movement

Each person gets a saved 3D model of their face, so turning, talking or looking away doesn't throw the gaze.

Every grasp from pixels

Four stickers on the 25 cm board calibrate the camera. No tape measure, no answer key.

Five SG90 servos: base, shoulder, elbow, wrist roll and claw. Face electrodes: jaw, temple and both eye corners.

(2:50–3:25) Four decisions we're proud of. First, the hum on our headset throws a short burst once a second that looks just like a clench. So a clench only counts if it's held for 0.7 seconds and both the jaw and temple electrodes see it; hum bursts and talking don't pick anything. Second, webcam eye trackers fall apart when you move your head. Ours saves a 3D model of each person's face and learns what head movement does, and it ignores you while you're talking or looking away. Third, we didn't redraw our arm for the simulator: we read the team's CAD file directly, found every joint from the pins the parts share, and modelled the geared claw. Fourth, every grasp still comes from pixels: four stickers on the base board calibrate the camera, so nobody measures anything.
