We’d love introductions to ALS clinics and occupational therapists so we can test with the people this is for. Thank you — happy to take questions. Q&A prep (likely judge questions): • Why not SSVEP? We tried it for two days: 185 trials, hum about 1,000× the signal, best 32% on 7 targets. We measured, then changed course. The analysis is in our paper. • Is a jaw clench really a BCI? It's muscle (EMG), read by an EEG headset. We say so plainly: we picked the signal that works for the user. • Why use the headset if the webcam tracks the eyes? The webcam does all the eye tracking; the headset is the "yes". If looking alone picked things, anything you rested your eyes on would move the arm. A clench lets you keep looking at the object while you confirm, and the camera and the jaw electrodes must both agree before anything moves. • How accurate is the eye tracker? First real profile: median 229 px error across 9 dots on a laptop screen. So we zoom in on the objects, pick the nearest one, and always confirm with a clench. It corrects its drift after every pick. • What if someone can't clench, e.g. bulbar ALS? Any switch that sends a key press works, and the eye-corner glance backup needs no clench. Next: a blink or eyebrow switch. • What about talking or looking away? The webcam sees the jaw moving or the head turned, and the clench is ignored. • How is this different from existing assistive arms? They're steered by joystick or voice. Reach needs only eye gaze and a clench, and the arm finds the object itself. • What would you do with the prize funding? User testing with partners, a stronger shoulder servo and the safety review. • Is the code all from this weekend? Yes, all of it. The arm is printed and being assembled now.
