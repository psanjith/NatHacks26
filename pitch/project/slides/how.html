How it works

Five steps from a glance to a grasp

1

Camera sees the table

One fixed camera finds the objects from pixels alone.

2

Your eyes choose

A webcam tracks your gaze. That object turns yellow.

3

Hold a jaw clench

About a second, seen by jaw and temple electrodes.

4

Change your mind

Clench again within 1.5 s to cancel.

5

The arm fetches it

Locates it from the image, picks it up and places it.

Backup, in testing: glance left or right to step between objects, read by two electrodes at the eye corners.

(1:25–1:50) Here's the loop. A fixed camera sees the table and finds the objects from pixels alone. A webcam tracks your eyes, and the object you're looking at turns yellow. You hold a jaw clench for about a second; electrodes on the jaw muscle and the temple must both see it. You get a second and a half to change your mind with another clench. Then the arm locates the object from the camera image, picks it up and places it. If the webcam can't see your face, glancing left or right steps between objects instead, using two electrodes at the corners of the eyes. That backup is built and still being tested.
