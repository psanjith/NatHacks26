How it works

Five steps from a glance to a grasp

1

Camera sees the table

One fixed camera. Objects found from pixels only.

2

Each object flickers

At 7.5, 8.57, 10 or 12 Hz, right on the object.

3

EEG reads the choice

The brain echoes the frequency you look at.

4

A jaw clench confirms

Or cancels. No clench in 1.5 s means yes.

5

The arm fetches it

Locates it from the image, plans, picks and places.

One driver interface: the same software runs the simulator, our printed arm, or a commercial assistive arm.

(1:10–1:40) Here’s the loop. One fixed camera sees the table and finds the objects from pixels alone. Each object gets a patch flickering at its own frequency, drawn right on the object. The user looks at the one they want, and their visual cortex echoes that frequency, which we read from three EEG electrodes over the back of the head. A short confirmation follows: a jaw clench cancels, no clench means yes. Then the arm locates the object from the same image, plans the motion, picks it up and places it. All of this sits behind one driver interface, so the same software runs our simulator, our printed arm, or a commercial assistive arm.
