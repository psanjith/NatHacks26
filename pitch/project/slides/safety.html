Designed for its users

Nothing moves by accident, and nothing flashes

Safety

  • A clench counts only if held and seen by two electrodes
  • Ignored while you talk or look away
  • 1.5 s to cancel; Esc stops the arm at once
  • Printed arm: a hardware stop button and a timeout if the laptop goes quiet [planned]

Accessibility

  • No flickering lights, so no seizure risk
  • Eye gaze is how many people with ALS already communicate [cite]
  • Clench too weak? Any switch that sends a key press works
  • Calibrate once per person (about 45 s), then one dot per session
(3:50–4:15) We designed for the people who'll actually use this. Nothing moves by accident: a clench only counts if it's held and both electrodes see it, it's ignored while you're talking or looking away, you get a second and a half to cancel, and Escape stops the arm at once. On the printed arm we're adding a hardware stop button and a timeout if the laptop goes quiet. And nothing flashes: moving away from flickering lights also removed the seizure risk that came with them. Eye gaze is already how many people with ALS communicate. Jaw strength can fade in bulbar ALS, so any switch that sends a key press can stand in for the clench. Each person calibrates once, in about 45 seconds, and after that it's a single dot.
