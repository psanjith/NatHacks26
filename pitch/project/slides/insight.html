Our idea

The brain chooses.
The robot moves.

A non-invasive EEG headset can’t steer every joint of an arm. But choosing 1 of 4 objects takes only 2 bits, and that is something a visual BCI (SSVEP) can do.

So the camera turns whatever is on the table into the options. The scene is the menu. The arm does all the moving.

(0:45–1:10) Our key insight: a consumer EEG headset can’t steer every joint of an arm — that’s why most brain-controlled arms are slow and frustrating. But choosing one of four objects is only two bits, and steady-state visual evoked potentials, SSVEP, are one of the most dependable non-invasive brain signals for exactly that kind of choice. So we flip it: the camera turns whatever is on the table into the menu, the user just chooses, and the robot handles all the movement.
