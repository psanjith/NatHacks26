P.N.J.E.X ORIGINAL V6: DYNAMIC GRASP RESULTS AND LIMITS
Final formal-result inventory: 44 integrations; MuJoCo 4.0.0

RESULT

No tested case passed all of the declared acceptance criteria. The 60 mm,
100 g sphere fell in every tested sphere case. The 60 mm diameter x 80 mm,
100 g cylinder, initially aligned with the palm axis, could remain grasped and
be lifted in several numerical scenarios, but it first moved approximately
15-18 mm relative to the gripper in the basic winding/friction sweep. That
exceeds the 5 mm post-release slip requirement, so these are FAIL results,
including the cases that look stable during the final three-second hold.

The representative cylinder case produced a minimum hold lift of 53.579 mm
and final-hold slip of 0.726 mm, but maximum slip from release was 15.263 mm.
At half the timestep, these values were 52.804 mm, 1.038 mm and 15.884 mm.
Increasing the assumed micro-servo torque cap to 0.10 N.m reduced release slip
to 14.107 mm; it still failed. Neither a working animation nor a stable final
pose should be presented as meeting the original slip requirement.

These outcomes describe the specified numerical experiments. They do not prove
that every possible parameter choice or object placement fails, and they do
not establish the physical load capacity of the printed hardware. They also
do not support a claim that the gripper can hold arbitrary 100 g objects.

The original SOLIDWORKS V6 geometry was not redesigned for these tests.

WHAT WAS MODELED

The model uses the original V6 component geometry and assembly transforms,
with four main arm joints, three independently driven cable spools and six
passive finger joints. The four main drives represent SF3218MG servos; the
three spool drives represent MG90S-compatible / ROB-009 micro servos. The user
confirmed purchasing four SF3218MG units. The quantity of purchased micro
servos has not been confirmed; three in the simulation is a model requirement,
not an assertion that three have already been bought.

The native visual assembly contains 567 component instances and 72 unique
part meshes. Explicit link masses, centers of mass and inertia tensors are
aggregated from the modeled parts. Native CAD solid geometry supplies shape
integrals; material density and purchased-component mass assumptions supply
the physical mass values. Reference motor/hardware solids are simplified CAD
representations, not scans of the purchased items.

The main mass scenario treats PETG parts as uniform solid material at
1,250 kg/m^3. One cylinder sensitivity case scales only those PETG masses and
inertias to 0.75 of that scenario. This is NOT a 75% slicer infill setting,
and it does not identify the mass distribution of a real print. Actual spring
wire, tendon, adhesive, wiring and some assembly additions remain unmeasured.
The CAD spring-coil envelope solids are clearance references and contribute
neither collision forces nor physical spring mass. Real springs are not
assumed to weigh zero; their mass has not been identified.

Gravity is 9.80665 m/s^2. The test objects are homogeneous rigid bodies:
  Sphere:   mass 0.100 kg; radius 0.030 m; principal inertia 3.6e-5 kg.m^2.
  Cylinder: mass 0.100 kg; radius 0.030 m; length 0.080 m;
            transverse inertia 7.58333e-5 kg.m^2,
            axial inertia 4.5e-5 kg.m^2.
These are explicit test-object assumptions, not hollow-cup properties.

The base is fixed to the world. This represents an adequately secured base
and mounting board. Clamp stiffness, board flexure, fastener pullout, table
motion and overturning of an unsecured assembly were not simulated. A real
base must be secured before these experiments can be compared with hardware.

Collision surfaces use convex pieces derived from the CAD geometry; visual
meshes alone are not used as evidence of contact clearance. Collision coverage
includes these 20 part templates and all their applicable instances:
  01 Base Pedestal; 02 Yaw Servo Tray; 03 Waist Turntable;
  04 Upper Arm Link; 05 Forearm Link; 06 Compact Wrist Adapter;
  21 Compact Palm; 22 Rear Servo Carrier; 23 Root Fork;
  24 Proximal Finger; 25 Distal Finger; 26 Proximal Soft Pad;
  27 Distal Soft Pad; 30 Idler Fork;
  40 Shoulder Support; 41 Elbow Support; 42 Wrist Support;
  SF3218MG reference body; MG90S-compatible reference body;
  plywood mounting-board reference.

Small fasteners, washers, bearings, shaft sleeves, horns, some posts/guards,
spools/idlers, spring envelopes and the decorative badge are not individual
collision obstacles. Their modeled mass may still be included where specified
in mass_plan.json. Cable snagging on omitted hardware and internal bearing
contact are not covered. Adjacent connected body pairs have explicit collision
exclusions for their designed joint interfaces.

Convex decomposition can bridge small holes or slots. Sampled geometry/probe
checks are diagnostics, not a guaranteed maximum error bound. Rigid pad shapes
and compliant numerical contact do not reproduce actual TPU deformation.
The selected contact model uses normal and sliding friction; it does not add
independent rolling/torsional contact resistance.

Most sweeps used the lite_base collision variant, which simplifies only the
fixed base pedestal. Finger, pad, palm and the other 19 collision templates
are unchanged. The representative cylinder's full and lite model integrations
had exactly equal recorded time, qpos, telemetry and final_qpos arrays. The
baseline sphere full/lite comparison also preserved the failure result and
key metrics. This validates that substitution for those checked trajectories,
not every possible collision involving the base.

CABLES, SPRINGS AND CONTROLS

Each finger has one pull-only cable crossing its proximal and distal joints.
The six finger joints are passive: no hidden position motors or finger-angle
locks force the grasp. Each cable has a real generalized reaction on its
8 mm effective-radius spool shaft. With extension e, stiffness k and damping c:
  e = current routed length + radius * spool rotation - free cable length
  T = 0 when e <= 0; otherwise T = max(0, k*e + c*extension_rate)
  finger/body cable forces = -transpose(length Jacobian) * T
  spool reaction torque = -radius * T
Thus the line can go slack, stretch, pull and backdrive the spool. It cannot
push. Cable tension is not independently clipped to a convenient holding
force; the finite servo torque and spool dynamics determine it. Brief dynamic
tension peaks may exceed the static torque/radius value.

The spatial route follows the CAD guide and finger sites. Its evaluated length
derivatives have the closing sign over the modeled finger ranges. The proximal
moment arm is approximately 6.00-6.50 mm and the distal moment arm is
6.00-8.20 mm. Ideal cable guides are frictionless; a sampled guide route is not
a measurement of pulley friction or a capstan-friction model. No arbitrary
transmission-efficiency multiplier was used.

Baseline cable assumptions are k = 1,500 N/m, c = 0.1 N.s/m and 1 mm initial
slack. The baseline spool armature is 1e-6 kg.m^2. These are unmeasured scenario
inputs, not specifications of a selected line, knot or purchased servo.

Each finger has opening torsion springs at both joints. The implemented
baseline stiffnesses are 0.005 N.m/rad proximally and 0.0222 N.m/rad distally,
with opening preloads of 21.8492 and 19.2466 degrees. The spring reference
angles are negative; the preload is active at the open pose. These values
come from the original design calculation, approximately 0.004946 and
0.021946 N.m/rad before the runner's stated rounding/scenario choice.
They are not calibrated measurements of purchased springs. The geometric
finger ranges are 0-10 degrees proximal and 0-60 degrees distal, enforced by
soft numerical stops rather than hard angle overwrites.

There are seven torque-controlled motors: four main joints and three spools.
Baseline assumed shaft caps are 1.5 N.m per main servo and 0.06 N.m per spool
servo. The latter corresponds to 7.5 N static cable tension at 8 mm radius.
These caps are hypotheses, not manufacturer continuous-duty ratings.

The main controller uses Kp = 50 N.m/rad and Kd = 0.6 N.m.s/rad; the spool
controller uses Kp = 0.4 and Kd = 0.0015 in the same units. Main-joint model
bias-force compensation is added to feedback and then clipped by the same
torque cap. It is ideal model-based assistance at the arm joints, not a direct
support force on the free object. It does not supply an attached-payload
constraint or a hidden holding force. The modeled gains do not reproduce the
unknown internal controller or PWM response of the purchased servos.

No measured torque-speed curve, voltage sag, current limit, gear backlash,
thermal behavior or continuous-duty limit was identified. Constant torque
caps alone do not establish that the hardware can sustain the simulated
torque at its simulated speed for three seconds or longer.

PUBLISHED DATA VERSUS ASSUMPTIONS

The supplier data reviewed for this study state:
  SunFounder SF3218MG: 4.8-7.2 V; advertised stall torque 20.5-22.8 kgf.cm;
  no-load speed range 0.18-0.14 s/60 degrees; mass 56 g +/-5%.
  The reviewed product page does not assign those endpoint values to 6 V,
  or establish current draw and continuous operating torque.

  BC Robotics ROB-009: 4.8-6 V; stall torque 1.8 kgf.cm at 4.8 V and
  2.2 kgf.cm at 6 V; speeds 0.10 and 0.08 s/60 degrees respectively.

  The original TowerPro MG90S page is a separate reference: it associates
  2.2 kgf.cm with 6.6 V. Its 13.4 g mass is a proxy in this model, not a
  measured mass or proof that the purchased ROB-009 is identical.

Published stall figures were not relabeled as safe continuous torque. Friction,
cable stiffness/slack/damping, actual spring rate/preload, joint friction,
rotor inertia, contact compliance and control gains remain assumptions.

EXPERIMENT AND FINAL ACCEPTANCE RULES

Each formal run integrates eight seconds of dynamics:
  0.0-2.5 s: an explicitly declared temporary fixture prepositions the object.
             The spools close smoothly from 0.3 to 2.2 s.
  2.5 s:     permanently release the fixture; record the free object's pose
             and linear/angular velocity. Do not reset its state afterward.
  2.5-3.0 s: unsupported settling at the initial arm target.
  3.0-5.0 s: lift with a smooth quintic arm target.
  5.0-8.0 s: hold the final target for three full seconds.

The engineering arm angles change from [0, 60, -85, -10] degrees to
[0, 80, -80, -25] degrees in yaw/shoulder/elbow/wrist order. The commanded
increments are [0, +20, +5, -15] degrees. Actual motion comes from integration
of finite torques, gravity, springs, cables and contacts; recorded trajectories
are not an animation created by prescribing every pose.

This is a pre-positioned-object grasp-and-lift test. It is not an autonomous
tabletop acquisition or approach-path demonstration. The fixture phase cannot
be counted as an unsupported grasp.

The final implemented criteria require ALL of the following:
  - Finite states, no MuJoCo warnings, and permanent fixture release.
  - Initial object penetration at most 0.1 mm.
  - Actual free-object height at least 50 mm above release height throughout
    the final hold, for a completed hold duration of at least 3.0 seconds.
  - Maximum palm-relative object-COM slip from release at most 5 mm, including
    settling, lift and hold; final-hold relative slip also at most 5 mm.
  - No post-release object contact with the floor or other non-whitelisted
    support geometry. The palm/finger whitelist is explicit; the entire arm
    is not automatically an allowed support.
  - Gripper contact during at least 99% of hold steps; contact with at least
    two moving fingers during at least 95%; soft-pad contact during at least
    95%. This does not by itself prove three-finger force closure.
  - Maximum object penetration at most 0.8 mm, separately checked after
    release and over the entire cycle; robot self-penetration at most 1 mm.
  - Every limited hinge stays within its nominal limits plus a declared
    0.5-degree numerical overshoot tolerance.
  - No direct generalized force on the payload free joint, and post-release
    arm target tracking error at most 3 degrees.

These are the final numerical gates. Older preliminary notes suggesting a
0.5 mm contact threshold or a 45 mm hold-height allowance are not the executed
acceptance rules. Slip, support contacts, penetration and joint limits are
checked every integration step and at the final endpoint, not only in video
frames or the 10 ms telemetry samples.

There is no hidden object stabilization or support force after release. One
explicit exception to the zero-external-disturbance baseline is
cylinder_range_disturb_02N: a declared 0.2 N world-Y force is applied to the
payload from 6.0 to 6.2 s through xfrc_applied. It tests that particular push;
it is not a support force or evidence of disturbance resistance in all
directions. The payload's direct qfrc_applied components remain zero.

FORMAL RESULTS AND SENSITIVITY

The results directory contains 44 completed integrations: 18 sphere and
26 cylinder records, including repeated full/lite and timestep checks. These
are not 44 independent hardware tests. All records have status FAIL; there
are no strict PASS_CONDITIONAL results and no engine-warning results.
The total is 736,000 integration steps. Old partial-collision/debug trials
are excluded from these conclusions.

Representative axial cylinder: z = 60 mm in the palm frame, winding target
1.2 rad, pad friction coefficient 0.5, main cap 1.5 N.m, micro cap 0.06 N.m.
  Minimum hold lift:                   53.5788 mm
  Maximum slip from release:           15.2627 mm  (FAIL)
  Maximum slip during final hold:       0.7257 mm
  Maximum post-release penetration:     0.3511 mm
  Maximum whole-cycle penetration:      0.6423 mm
  Maximum hinge-limit overshoot:        0.2344 degrees
  Contact fraction for each finger:     1.0 throughout the hold
  Palm support contact:                 none
  Mean vertical object contact force:   0.980709 N
  Object weight:                        0.980665 N
  Peak main torques, yaw to wrist:     [0.0551, 1.1612, 0.8120, 0.3519] N.m
  Peak micro torques:                  [0.05983, 0.06000, 0.06000] N.m
  Peak cable tensions:                 [7.4792, 7.7114, 7.7118] N

The second and third spool controls were at the assumed cap throughout the
hold. These are requested/model torques, not measured electrical or thermal
loads. The contact force approximately balancing weight and the permanently
released payload establish a dynamic contact-supported hold in this scenario;
they do not erase the earlier slip failure. Fixture drift was about 0.00126 mm,
with near-zero release linear velocity, rather than a large preloaded jump.

The six basic cylinder winding/friction combinations (winding 0.8/1.2/1.6 rad,
friction 0.5/0.8) all retained the object during the final hold but failed
release slip, with approximately 15.26-18.11 mm settling/movement. Increasing
the micro cap to 0.10 N.m yielded lift 55.202 mm and hold slip 0.262 mm, but
release slip remained 14.107 mm. This is still a failed strict test.

The one-variable cylinder ranges additionally tested spring scale 0.8/1.2,
preload offsets -8/+8 degrees, cable stiffness 500/5,000 N/m, slack 0/2 mm,
and finger dry friction 0.001/0.003 N.m. Separate cases tested micro caps
0.03/0.10 N.m, main cap 1.0 N.m, spool armature 1e-5 kg.m^2, the declared
0.2 N push, and PETG mass scale 0.75. None passed all criteria. For example,
the 500 N/m cable and 0.03 N.m micro-cap cases also missed the 50 mm lift and
exceeded the hinge-limit tolerance. The 1.0 N.m main-cap case lost arm height
and contacted the floor. Lower modeled print mass did not remove slip failure.

The sphere trials include palm-axis centers at 40, 60 and 80 mm, selected
winding/friction combinations, and eight single-variable spring/preload/cable
stiffness/slack changes around the z60, winding 1.2 rad, friction 0.8 case.
All tested spheres fell. The failed sphere's eventual long floor-rolling
distance is not a meaningful prediction of real tabletop travel.

Contact inspection of the baseline sphere just before release found three
actual distal-pad contacts near the pad ends. Their normal components along
the palm's outward axis were approximately -0.563 to -0.583: they pushed
toward the palm rather than directly away from it. The sphere then moved
backward/laterally and changed to mixed pad/finger-skeleton contacts. This is
evidence that object placement and contact geometry deserve review; increasing
servo torque alone is not a demonstrated solution. This observation does not
prove failure for every possible pad material, position or object shape.

The world-upright cylinder case started with 7.1536 mm of geometric overlap,
well above the 0.1 mm initial-placement gate. Its result is retained to expose
the setup defect but EXCLUDED from claims about the grasp capability of a
correctly placed upright cylinder. A valid upright placement remains untested.

The sensitivity blocks vary one parameter at a time around specific baselines.
The small geometry/winding/friction sweeps are selected combinations. They are
not a full factorial search, uncertainty distribution, optimization proof or
coverage of arbitrary off-center placements, object sizes and orientations.

NUMERICAL CHECKS AND THEIR LIMITS

Baseline timestep is 0.0005 s, using implicitfast integration and the Newton
solver. The custom cable force is evaluated through the split step interface;
its explicit stiffness contribution still needs timestep sensitivity checks.

The representative sphere and cylinder were each rerun at 0.00025 s for the
full eight seconds. Both retained their prior result category and failed
criteria. The sphere first exceeded 5 mm slip at the 2.55 s telemetry sample
in both runs; onset timing is resolved only to the 10 ms telemetry cadence.
Sphere maximum penetration changed by 0.00151 mm and maximum stop overshoot
by 0.11838 degrees. Its falling/rolling path was not required to be identical.

For the cylinder, half timestep changed minimum hold lift by -0.77454 mm and
hold slip by +0.31280 mm. The smaller-step result was lift 52.8042 mm,
hold slip 1.0385 mm and release slip 15.8842 mm. This is useful sensitivity
evidence, but two timesteps do not prove asymptotic convergence. The specified
additional 0.000125 s run was not triggered: neither behavior category changed
and the cylinder lift/hold-slip differences were each below 1 mm. Stiff-cable,
soft-stop and other range cases were not all independently timestep-refined.

The cable force implementation was checked against routed-length derivatives,
equal spool reaction and the continuous power/potential balance. That algebraic
check detects force-sign and missing-reaction mistakes; it is not a guarantee
of discrete-time energy conservation or physical material accuracy.

The simulation does not validate printed-layer strength, fatigue, bolt or
horn retention, shaft bending, tendon abrasion/knots, actual spring seating,
cup crushing, pad deformation, object breakage, electrical safety margins,
current draw, power-supply capacity or motor heating. A successful rigid-body
simulation would not establish those properties either.

NECESSARY NEXT MEASUREMENTS AND REVIEWS

1. Confirm the micro-servo quantity and exact purchased variants, supply
   voltage and wiring. Measure available loaded torque/speed/current and
   temperature rise for the intended duty cycle; do not substitute stall
   torque for a sustainable rating.
2. Weigh the assembled moving links and actual hardware, and identify material,
   print orientation, infill distribution and fit. Update mass/COM/inertia
   assumptions rather than treating a mass scale as a slicer setting.
3. Measure each installed spring's torque-angle curve and preload; cable
   force-extension, slack and effective spool radius; hinge/guide friction.
   The current ideal guide assumption needs checking on the physical routing.
4. Measure pad/object friction and compliance for the intended sphere/cylinder
   surfaces. Check loaded contact locations, especially sphere pad-end contact
   and retreat toward the palm. Review the shape/contact arrangement against
   real assembly clearances before deciding whether any CAD change is needed.
5. Verify secure base mounting and the actual horn/shaft/fastener interfaces.
   This model fixes the foundation and does not verify clamp or print strength.
6. Establish a collision-free initial placement for the upright cylinder.
   Repeat a low-height, controlled hardware release/lift/hold experiment while
   measuring slip from release, rather than resetting the slip reference after
   the object has settled. Retain failed trials and compare measured behavior
   with the simulation before using it for design decisions.
